§THE ARGUMENT

Clinicians are not struggling because they do not know enough. They are struggling because there is not enough time to do what they already know properly.

You already know what good care looks like. You trained for years to deliver it. The problem is not your knowledge or your commitment. The problem is everything else that fills your day before you get to use either of those things properly.

AI does not fix the NHS. It does not solve workforce pressures or give you back the hours that were never there to begin with.

But it can speed up the parts of your job that should not be taking as long as they do. And for a lot of clinicians that time adds up to something meaningful.

WHO WRITES IT

I am Juwon.

NHS Occupational Therapist
East London

I started Clinically Intelligent during time away from the ward. I had been thinking about it for a while, but it took stepping back from the day to day to actually build it.

I was using AI tools in my own work and they were making a difference. Not in a theoretical way. In a I finished my notes before the end of shift way. In a I found a guideline update my Trust had not circulated way. In a I had more mental space left at the end of a Tuesday way.

Most of the people around me had not been shown how to use these tools. That gap was not going to close by itself. So I started writing about it. For people like me. Clinicians who are curious, who want to stay ahead, and who do not have time to go through generic AI content that was not written for someone with a caseload.

Every tool I cover I have tried myself first. Every scenario comes from real clinical work. If it did not earn its place, it does not make the newsletter.

Over the last six years in the NHS, I have built services, led teams, and been part of work recognised within Barts Health, including the Newham Frailty Virtual Ward, which won a 2024 WeImprove Award for Working Across Systems.

Before healthcare I came through the academy systems at Stevenage and Leyton Orient. I never made a career out of football, but I carried one lesson with me: performance is often the result of small improvements applied consistently over time. That idea sits underneath Clinically Intelligent.
